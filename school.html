Personal Security Risk Assessment

I assessed five devices on a small home network and presented the results in an executive memo. I built an asset inventory and network diagram, used the desktop Security Risk Assessment (SRA) Tool to document findings, and separated questions specific to healthcare from risks that applied to the network.

What the assessment looked at

I evaluated whether existing safeguards could protect stored data and whether backups could support recovery after a disruption.

What I recommended

I recommended backing up more often, testing file restores, evaluating drive encryption, and making security monitoring more reliable.

How the CIS Controls help

A risk assessment helps decide what needs attention first. The CIS Controls give teams concrete safeguards to work toward. Control 1 connects to the asset inventory, Control 3 to protecting stored data, Control 8 to collecting and reviewing logs, and Control 11 to backing up data and testing recovery. These are examples of how the findings map to safeguards.

Why this matters in practice

Using a small network let me practice the same process I would bring to an organization: identify what needs protection, explain the impact if something goes wrong, and give leadership practical next steps. For example, a successful backup job tells me a copy was made. A restore test tells me whether the team can actually recover what it needs.

Instructor feedback: The assessment was commended for identifying assets and associated risks, categorizing risk appropriately, and presenting a strong executive summary for the customer.

Vulnerability Assessment & Security Analysis with Nessus

As part of a hands-on cybersecurity assignment, Erika performed vulnerability assessments using Nessus within a Kali Linux virtual machine environment. The project involved scanning multiple operating systems and intentionally vulnerable machines to identify security weaknesses, analyze vulnerabilities, and research associated CVEs (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures).

This project strengthened practical experience with:

• Vulnerability scanning and reporting
• Kali Linux administration
• Security analysis and remediation concepts
• CVE research and risk identification
• Troubleshooting and lab environment configuration

The assignment provided valuable exposure to the types of tools and methodologies commonly used within cybersecurity operations and vulnerability management programs.

Cybersecurity Competition & Team Defense Operations

While serving as Cybersecurity Club President, Erika participated in a team-based cybersecurity competition focused on defending enterprise infrastructure in a simulated attack environment. Teams were responsible for securing servers, desktops, services, and network infrastructure while responding to real-time security threats and operational challenges.

The competition emphasized collaboration, rapid problem-solving, system hardening, patch management, and defensive security operations under pressure. With limited preparation time and incomplete information about the environment, participants had to quickly assess risks, secure systems, and maintain operational stability as a team.

This experience reinforced the importance of communication, adaptability, and teamwork within cybersecurity operations while providing practical exposure to real-world defensive security concepts.
